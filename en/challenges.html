Challenges Encountered and Strategies Adopted

During the development of the project, several practical difficulties emerged, requiring iterative verification and correction activities.

  • Construction of the integrated dataset: The phase of merging the different open data sources required particular attention. Some datasets had different column names to represent similar information, while others contained geographical data expressed in non-uniform formats. It was therefore necessary to perform normalization and harmonization operations before proceeding with the generation of the final Knowledge Graph.
  • Management of geographical information: The datasets used contained both point coordinates and polygonal geometries expressed through GeoShape fields. It was therefore necessary to define a coherent representation of this information within the ontology, distinguishing between geographical coordinates and more complex spatial geometries.
  • Definition of ontological properties: It was necessary to correctly distinguish between datatype properties and object properties, identifying when it was appropriate to reuse properties already present in national ontologies and when to introduce new local properties instead. The modeling of the relationship between porticoes and urban points of interest required a specific in-depth study, leading to the definition of the hostsPOI and isUnderPortico properties.
  • Ontology construction in Protégé and visualization: In an initial phase, problems occurred in importing and visualizing the extended ontology, as locally defined classes were not immediately visible within the ontological hierarchy. Resolving the problem required a verification of the IRIs used, the import relationships with the national POI-AP_IT ontology, and the ways files were opened and saved in the different formats supported by Protégé.
  • Automatic generation via LLMs: During the generation of RDF triples, some inconsistencies emerged in the produced outputs. In several cases, the models tended to use properties not present in the ontology, datatypes inconsistent with those defined, or classes not perfectly aligned with the designed structure. For this reason, a manual verification of the generated triples was performed, accompanied by a progressive refinement of the prompts used in the experiments.
  • Verification of semantic consistency and graph queryability: The first activity concerned the verification of the semantic consistency of the graph using the HermiT reasoner. During this phase, some inconsistencies were detected regarding the POIID property, defined in the national ontology with an xsd:integer range. In the triples generated by the ChatGPT Plus language model, however, this property had been valued using descriptive strings (for example, "Arredo urbano_0447"), causing a violation of the semantic constraints provided by the ontology. The analysis of the explanations provided by the reasoner allowed for quick identification of the origin of the problem and correction of the non-compliant triples. After removing the incompatible values, the HermiT reasoner successfully completed the inference process without highlighting further inconsistencies, confirming the consistency of the resulting Knowledge Graph.

    Subsequently, the queryability of the graph was verified using SPARQL queries. Initially, this activity was carried out using the SPARQL plugin integrated into Protégé. However, when opening the SPARQL view on the populated Knowledge Graph, the software generated compatibility errors related to the query plugin, preventing the execution of queries directly within the Protégé environment and requiring the adoption of alternative tools.
