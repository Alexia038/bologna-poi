Ontology Extension

Proposal for a Local Ontological Extension in Protégé

Following the analysis of the national POI-AP_IT ontology and its comparison with the open datasets of the Municipality of Bologna, the necessity to extend the existing vocabulary emerged in order to represent certain categories of urban points of interest not explicitly modeled in the reference ontology. For this reason, a local ontology named bologna-poi was designed and implemented using the Protégé editor, identified by the IRI http://dati.comune.bologna.it/ontology/bologna-poi and based on the reuse of concepts defined in the national ontology.

The extension was carried out following an incremental and conservative approach, aimed at ensuring maximum interoperability with the national model. In particular, the BolognaUrbanPointOfInterest class was introduced, defined as a subclass of PointOfInterest, serving as the local superclass for the representation of urban points of interest in the city of Bologna.

Starting from this class, the main categories present in the municipal datasets were defined, namely UrbanFurniture, BusStop, ParkingArea, PublicToilet, and Portico. The UrbanFurniture class was further specialized into the subclasses Bench, Bin, and Fountain, in order to distinguish the different types of urban furniture available in the source data.

Once the class hierarchy was defined, the activity focused on property modeling. A principle of systematic reuse of properties already available in the imported national ontologies was adopted, utilizing elements such as name, identifier, description, fullAddress, URL, and other consolidated descriptive properties wherever possible. Only in cases where the existing vocabulary was insufficient to represent the information contained in the municipal datasets were new local datatype properties introduced.

These include, for example, specific properties for certain subclasses, such as BusStopCode, BusLine, and BusStopName for bus stops, ParkingCapacity and Tariff for parking areas, Material and StructureType for urban furniture, and LISVideoGuide and opens for structures equipped with additional information on timetables and accessibility. For each property, appropriate domains and ranges were defined using standard XML Schema datatypes such as xsd:string, xsd:integer, xsd:boolean, xsd:date, and xsd:anyURI.

Particular attention was also dedicated to modeling the semantic relations between entities. For this purpose, two specific object properties were introduced. The hostsPOI property was defined to represent the fact that a Portico can host one or more urban points of interest. The domain of the property was thus associated with the Portico class, while the range was defined as BolognaUrbanPointOfInterest. Complementarily, the isUnderPortico property was introduced, used to express the inverse relationship from the point of interest to the portico under which it is located. This choice allows for the explicit representation of a distinctive feature of the Bolognese urban context, highlighting the role of porticoes as a structural and spatial element of the city.

The resulting ontology therefore constitutes a local extension of the national model, capable of preserving its semantic compatibility while simultaneously providing a more detailed representation that aligns with the characteristics of the urban points of interest present in the municipal territory of Bologna.
